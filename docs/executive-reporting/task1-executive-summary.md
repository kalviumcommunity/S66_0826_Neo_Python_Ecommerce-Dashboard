# CHURN REDUCTION INITIATIVE
## Executive Summary

### Situation

Customer churn is our leading revenue loss driver, costing **$2M annually**. Our current churn rate is 7% versus an industry average of 4%. We analyzed root causes across 50,000 customers over 24 months and identified one high-impact, low-cost fix.

### Key Findings

- **Support speed directly drives retention.** Customers answered within 2 hours churn at 3%. Customers waiting over 24 hours churn at 12% — a 4x difference.
- **Our 6-hour average response is the bottleneck.** We are answering at a speed that puts the typical customer in the 9% churn zone.
- **High-value customers are most sensitive.** Accounts spending over $10K/year churn at 15% when support is slow — we are losing our biggest customers fastest.

### Business Risks

- **Revenue at risk:** At 7% churn we lose $2M every year, and the high-value segment is most exposed.
- **Competitive vulnerability:** Better-supported rivals are recruiting our frustrated customers; winning one back costs 5x what retaining them would have.
- **Worsening trend:** Support ticket volume is up 40% year over year while response times have degraded. Without action, churn accelerates on its own.

### Recommendations

1. **Hire 2 support engineers** — Cost: $200K/year. Cuts average response from 6 hours to under 2. Expected to recover **$400K** in churn reduction; ROI of 2x within the first year.
2. **Implement a 2-hour response SLA** — Cost: $0. Document the target, track it daily, publish results. Creates accountability. Live Jan 1.
3. **Priority support lane for high-value customers** — Cost: $50K engineering. Routes $10K+/year accounts to a sub-1-hour lane. Expected to halve high-value churn within 60 days. Live Feb 1.

### Decision Needed

Approve **$200K hiring budget and $50K engineering spend by Dec 15** — a total of $250K to recover $400K in year one and stop the $2M annual leak. Every quarter of delay costs roughly $500K in customers we already know how to keep.
