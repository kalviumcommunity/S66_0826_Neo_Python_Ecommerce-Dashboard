# Task 6 (Bonus): Same Finding, Three Audiences

All three versions rest on the identical core finding: **first response time drives churn (3% at <2h vs 12% at >24h), we currently average 6h, and closing the gap recovers ~$400K/year for $250K.** Only the emphasis changes.

---

## Version A: Board of Directors
*Strategic risk and shareholder value. Financial metrics only. One paragraph.*

> Churn runs at 7% against an industry average of 4%, a **$2M annual revenue leak** concentrated in our highest-value accounts, which churn at 15%. Root-cause analysis ties 40% of the stay/leave difference to one controllable operational metric: support response speed. Management requests approval of a **$250K initiative that returns an estimated $400K in year one (2x ROI)**, converts a worsening trend into a competitive moat, and stabilizes the ~$10M high-value revenue block. Unaddressed, ticket growth of 40% YoY implies further degradation; each quarter of delay costs approximately $500K. We will report churn and response metrics quarterly beginning Q2.

## Version B: Operations Team
*Implementation detail and timeline. Process changes. Two paragraphs.*

> We are making support response speed a managed operational metric with a hard target: **first response under 2 hours, currently averaging 6.1 hours**. Three changes start Jan 1: (1) daily response-time reporting published every morning at 9am — this becomes the standing item in the ops review; (2) two additional support engineers join between Jan and Apr, so build them into capacity planning and shift schedules now; (3) from Feb 1, accounts over $10K/year route automatically to a priority queue with a 1-hour target — update your escalation playbooks and triage rules to reflect the new lane before go-live.

> Ownership is concrete: Ops leads SLA tracking and staffing; a named workstream owner per change by Dec 20; Finance sign-off Dec 20; hiring posted Dec 1. Escalation thresholds, queue-routing rules, and dashboard access will be reviewed with team leads in the Dec 15 kickoff. Success at the Apr checkpoint means average response under 2 hours sustained for 60 consecutive days.

## Version C: Support Team
*Work environment, staffing plan, and support provided. Empathetic tone. Two paragraphs.*

> We hear you: ticket volume is up 40% this year and response times have stretched out — that is not a personal failing, it is a math problem, and leadership is fixing the math. **Two new team members are being hired starting Jan 1** to bring the workload per person back to a level where a 2-hour first response is actually achievable. The new 2-hour standard is a target the company is staffing to meet, not a whip to crack you with — you will have the headcount and tools to hit it.

> You will also get help, not just pressure: a priority-routing system (live Feb 1) automatically sends the demanding high-value accounts to a dedicated lane so they stop landing unexpectedly in your queue mid-shift; a real-time response dashboard replaces manual tracking; and paid training on the new tools runs **Feb 1–14** during work hours. Team leads will collect your input on routing rules before it ships — the people doing the work design the workflow.
