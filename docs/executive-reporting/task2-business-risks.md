# Task 2: Business Risk Analysis

Each risk states **what it is (quantified), why it matters, and what action addresses it**.

---

## Risk 1: Revenue Loss From Churn

- **What:** 7% churn against 4% industry average = **$2M in lost annual revenue**, roughly $500K bleeding out every quarter.
- **Why It Matters:** This is our largest preventable revenue leak — preventable because 40% of the churn difference is explained by a single operational metric we control: how fast we send a first reply.
- **Action:** Response-speed improvements (Recommendations 1 and 2) move the average customer from the 9% churn zone to the 3% zone, recovering **~$400K/year** against a $200K cost.

## Risk 2: High-Value Customer Vulnerability

- **What:** Customers spending over $10K/year churn at **15% when support is slow**, more than double the 7% company average. This top 20% of accounts carries roughly $10M in contracted revenue.
- **Why It Matters:** Losing one $500K account wipes out more than two years of the proposed hiring investment. We are least fast exactly where we can least afford to be slow.
- **Action:** A dedicated priority lane with a sub-1-hour target for high-value accounts (Recommendation 3) — expected to halve their churn within 60 days at $50K one-time cost.

## Risk 3: Competitive Disadvantage

- **What:** Our exit interviews show slow support is the stated reason in 60%+ of surveyed churn events; those customers do not vanish, they move to better-supported rivals.
- **Why It Matters:** Acquisition costs far exceed retention — winning back a departed customer costs roughly **5x** what keeping them would have. Every frustrated customer is a referral to a competitor.
- **Action:** Fast, reliable first response becomes a **competitive moat**: it is visible in reviews and renewals, and it compounds while the underlying cost ($250K) is fixed.

## Risk 4: Operational Burnout Spiral

- **What:** Ticket volume is up **40% year over year** and average response time has degraded from 4 hours to 6.1 hours despite no headcount cuts — the classic signature of an overloaded team.
- **Why It Matters:** Overworked teams degrade further: mistakes rise, morale falls, support staff attrition follows, and each departure slows response times more, accelerating customer churn. The symptom (slow replies) and the disease (capacity) reinforce each other.
- **Action:** Hiring 2 engineers addresses the root cause rather than exhorting the current team to answer faster. The daily SLA dashboard also gives early warning of load spikes before they become missed targets.

---

## Cost of Inaction Summary

| Risk | Quantified exposure | Cost to address |
|---|---|---|
| Revenue loss from churn | $2M/year, worsening | $200K/year |
| High-value attrition | ~$10M segment at 15% churn | $50K one-time |
| Competitive erosion | 5x retention cost to win back | Included in above |
| Team burnout collapse | Response times worsening 40% YoY volume growth | Included in hiring |

**Doing nothing is not the cheap option.** The status quo costs $2M per year; the proposed response costs $250K.
